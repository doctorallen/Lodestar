# Decisions

The major gameplay decisions, newest last, and why each was made. Read this before changing one, and add new ones at the end.

## 2026-10-17: the founding interview

- **Top-down, not side-view.** Jumping and precise aiming are the hardest things to get right on touch controls. Walking in eight directions and mining the wall you face works with one thumb.
- **A persistent base plus roguelite dives.** Progress builds at home, while each dive is a fresh, seeded expedition. You get Terraria's long arc and short sessions to fit a phone.
- **Gear only, no block building.** All the depth goes into loot and crafting. Free building on a touch screen would cost a lot and add little to this game.
- **Crafting and Diablo-style loot together.** Crafted items roll affixes, exotic materials shape the rolls, found loot can be modified, and anything unwanted is salvaged. Crafting is never a fixed recipe and a drop is never wasted.
- **Death loses the haul, never the gear you're wearing.** This creates push-your-luck tension without wiping out hours of progress.
- **The player decides how deep to go, and extraction is at set points.** Leaving is a goal you have to reach, not a menu option.
- **Limited cargo.** It forces choices and gives you a reason to extract that has nothing to do with danger.
- **The pick's tier gates ore.** Seeing an ore you can't mine is the hook for the next craft.
- **Melee and magic, with spells in socketed cores.** Spells are a second layer of loot. A build can be melee only, magic only, or a balanced hybrid.
- **Optional bosses.** Bosses are loot piñatas at depth, never gates. The pick does the gating.
- **Rooms and corridors with ore veins, not fully destructible caves.** These are easier to read, design and balance.
- **Sealed relics are always strong.** Unidentified drops come only at Rare quality or above. The gamble is on which great item you got, never whether it's junk.
- **Rarity tiers, legendary powers and sets, with 2–4 affix lines per item.** Simple but interesting, and readable on a phone.
- **Danger rises with depth.** The further you push, the more the reason to leave builds up.
- **Magitech on an alien world.** A Starbound-style sci-fi world where the technology runs on magic.
- **The base is the Lodestar**, a fallen magnetic star over a sinkhole. Its lines are the extraction points, its pull weakening with depth is why the depths get more dangerous, and feeding it ore makes it grow. One idea explains three systems.
- **Upright first, sideways too.** It's built for one-handed upright play, with NEO's sideways layout as well.
- **A broad prototype first.** Every system in rough form across three biomes, to find what's fun before polishing.
- **NEO's tech approach.** Plain JavaScript, no build step, seeded generation, headless tests, and deploying to GitHub Pages.

## 2026-10-17: the art style

- **"Glow + Detail": D's detail in B's darkness.** Of the five styles compared, this one fits the setting and the push-your-luck feel best. Light shows you where to look, because ore, runes and lodelines glow in the dark. The 16px tiles keep 11–12 tiles across a phone screen. Dark rock tops with lit rims keep walls readable even where the light is low.

## 2026-10-17: the first playable loop

- **One button per hand.** A blade swings and a focus casts, each on its own held button. This keeps every build to the same few buttons, and makes melee, magic and hybrid builds simply a matter of what's held.
- **Cores fire from blades too.** In a focus, a core is a spell you cast for charge. In a blade, it fires free every third hit at reduced power. Cores stay worth finding for melee-only builds.
- **No off-hand ward yet.** Plate covers defense, and Ward Pulse is a core.
- **A pick breaks the ore above its own.** A Ferrite pick breaks Lumenite, and so on. The ore you can already mine always makes the key to the next.
- **The forge also makes cores, and the rune bench lives on the item card.** That means fewer screens to learn on a phone, and no separate Coreworks.
- **A Lodestone shard raises rarity rather than adding magnetism.** It gives one exotic a different and clearly useful job.
- **Cargo by weight:** ore and exotics weigh 1, gear and relics 3. Gear competes with ore without needing a grid inventory.
- **Rest heals.** Health returns slowly after a few seconds without being hurt. Without this, a bot that fought everything died on the first floor. Now danger builds with depth rather than with every fight.
- **The star's reach grows with it.** Each magnitude makes lodelines sure a floor deeper. Feeding the star makes deeper dives safer to try, as well as opening biomes.
- **Balance is checked by a bot.** The autopilot plays careers on every test run. It fails if a career can't bring a haul home early, improve its pick, or grow the star.

## 2026-10-17: feel, from the first time on a phone

- **Organic caves.** The designer found the room-and-corridor floors too blocky. Now there are lumpy caverns and tunnels that wander like rivers. Walls follow a smooth line through the tiles, rather than the tiles' squares, and bodies collide with that line, so what you see is what you bump into.
- **More alien.** Flora that glows, veins of light in the rock, specks in the ground, and less earthly biome colours.
- **Combat with timing.** Early fights were found unengaging, so the designer asked for:
  - combos and charged attacks (tap, tap, heavy; hold for a spin or a charged cast);
  - a dash with a moment of untouchability;
  - impact: hit-stop, shake, knockback, stagger and damage numbers;
  - enemies with readable tells, who take turns: circling crawlers that lunge, strafing wisps, constructs that show their charge line and are dazed by walls.
- **A visible weapon.** The blade is drawn swinging in the hand and the focus glowing over it, and the hand buttons show the held item's icon.
- **Start with a focus too.** Melee and range are both there from the first fight.
- **Hands are tapped, not held.** Holding now charges instead of repeating, which makes room for the charged attack.
- **Ore can be dropped like gear.** In the cargo screen it shows as rows you can drop by 1, 5 or all, and it lies as a heap to pick back up.
- **The character is the circuit diver**, chosen from seven concepts. Its suit's circuits and violet dome give its own light, and its drone is a separate sprite so it can follow.

## 2026-10-17: what the first phone sessions showed

- **Only ore may look like ore.** Glowing flora in ore colours was mistaken for ore. Now flora is dim and coloured unlike any ore, ground specks are gone, rock veins are faint, and ore twinkles.
- **No screen shake.** The designer disliked it. Hit-stop, knockback and numbers carry the impact.

## 2026-10-17: home is a place

- **A walkable outpost instead of a menu.** The designer found forging through menus uninteresting. The base is now the sinkhole's rim, with the star over the pit and stations to walk up to, and it visibly grows with the star.
- **Forging is played.** Three timed strikes on an anvil set the quality, which shifts the rarity odds, so skill nudges the dice without replacing them.
- **Arrivals are shown.** Forged pieces and opened relics rise in their rarity's light, and fed ore streams into the star.
- **Fewer trips home:** salvage in the field, ancient forges deep down, and every ride home feeds the star.
- **Real flora.** The lumps are replaced by designed sprites (mushrooms, stalactites, tube worms, roots…) that sway, sort in depth with the actors, and never look like ore.
- **Some flora fights back.** Snapjaws and spitters hide in the undergrowth, rooted, red where the harmless kind is green, pink or ember.

## 2026-10-17: from the research into similar games

The designer asked to build the top recommendations from a survey of about 35 comparable games:
- **The star's pull, per floor** (Spelunky's ghost, Dredge's panic). Visible pressure to keep moving, felt rather than read: the lamp dims, the dark stirs, and when the pull's gone the Hollow hunts and the lodeline goes dark.
- **A pocket** (Tarkov's secure container, Moonlighter). A fall loses the cargo but never everything. It grows with the star and is never sold.
- **Leaving takes a moment** (Dark and Darker). Extracting is a short channel that a hit or a step breaks, so you have to clear the way first. An emergency recall from anywhere costs more the deeper you are, so there's always a way home, at a price.
- **A phone-sized item card** (Diablo Immortal, Death Must Die): per-stat comparison, roll bars, a keep star, and salvage-all for downgrades.

## 2026-10-18: crafting is the heart of the game

The designer found that so much loot made forging pointless, and that forged items lacked variety. They asked to lean into crafting, and chose:
- **Blueprints plus parts.** Blueprints are item types with their own feel (spear, hammer, chain-whip, staff, gauntlet, weave, shell, lantern, drill). Each is made from an ore head, a creature-part binding and an optional exotic, so the choice of parts steers the result, and the anvil gives the temper.
- **Materials, not gear.** Enemies drop their own parts, graded by biome, so the forge is where gear comes from and different recipes send you hunting different creatures.
- **Elements from exotics:** shock, frost (which shatters), ember (which burns) and blight (which spreads).
- **Blueprints must be carried home** to be learned. Finding one is a push-your-luck moment.
- **More spells, and modifiers to combine them**: elemental (Frost Lance, Ember Burst, Chain Lightning, Thorn Ring), movement (Blink, Gravity Well, Phase Step), and modifier cores (Split, Amplify, Quicken, Chain, Echo) that share sockets with the spells, so a piece's sockets are a choice between more spells and better ones.

## 2026-10-18: a story, and a tighter outpost

- **Stranded.** The designer chose this story: the *Meridian* wrecked on the rim, a dying star whose shattered heart powers the way home, and three Shard Keepers at fixed depths. It gives the dives a purpose and an end.
- **Told lightly.** A five-panel opening, one-off lines from Wick, the star speaking as it grows, and eight echoes. Phone sessions are short, so there are no long cutscenes.
- **Shards are safe once taken.** Losing a story piece to a fall would be more frustrating than tense.
- **The outpost is compact,** with every station a few steps apart and the wreck at the south edge. The designer found things too far apart.
- **Enemies a little tougher:** more health and damage, more per room, more elites.

## 2026-10-18: expeditions, not depth (overnight team, round 1)

The designer said they weren't a fan of delving ever deeper in the same zone, and wanted meaningful progress in a 5-minute session. The balance run showed the bot ending 46 of 48 dives on depth 1, and the Keepers at depths 5–7 put the story 8+ minutes away.
- **An expedition is a chain of 4 sites.** Each site is a different zone (surface, cave, ruin; the last a Deep Vein or a Keeper's Lair). This replaces "the player decides how deep to go" and "danger rises with depth".
- **Two Ways On at each exit,** signposted with their zones, so the route is a choice.
- **The Keepers live in lairs at the 4th site,** offered as one of the ways while that biome's shard is missing. A shard is now one full expedition away.
- **The sunlit surface** is where every expedition starts. Its daylight is the star's pull: dusk as it fades, night and the Hollow when it's gone.
- **Shorter, richer sites:** about 75 s of pull each, and the ride home feeds the star 8 × site², so going one site further is worth a lot.

## 2026-10-18: a Tool slot (overnight team, round 1)

The designer asked for utility items that change the interface and play, such as showing the way to the nearest extraction.
- **One tool carried, chosen at the ledge.** A single slot makes it a choice per expedition (find the way home, find ore, see danger, map the site, carry more safely, or make an exit) rather than a pile of upgrades.
- **Tools are made once, from the stash,** gated by the star's magnitude. They're a materials sink for the parts that pile up, and another reason to feed the star.
- **The Lode Beacon** answers a site with no lodeline: an exit you build, at the cost of 6 noisy seconds. It can't bypass a Keeper.

## 2026-10-18: rifts (overnight team, round 1)

The designer asked for dimensional shifts and rifts that cause strange changes and variation to the map, above and below ground.
- **Four kinds to start:** graft (another biome's ground and ore bleeding through), ore wake, gate (paired tears), and inversion (daylight underground). They change the map, the loot or the route, not just the look.
- **Sealing is the push-your-luck moment,** for riftglass, which forges a piece a tier above its ore. That's a reason to engage with a rift rather than walk past.
- **Placed from the dive's seed after generation,** so floors still rebuild exactly and the golden hashes are untouched.
- **Not adopted yet** from the designer's longer list: phase seams (walls that flicker), negative space, gravity wells, time slips. They're harder to read on a phone screen; worth a second round.

## 2026-10-18: an expedition survives the tab closing (overnight team, round 1)

Phones drop background tabs often. The run is now saved on arriving at each site and whenever the page is hidden, and offered back at the outpost on the next load: CARRY ON (from that site's entry, with the cargo, health and pull it had, and mined veins still gone) or GIVE IT UP (the cargo is lost, the pocket comes home). Enemies and drops on the site come back fresh. Since health and pull are saved too, closing the tab mid-fight doesn't buy much.

## 2026-10-18: contracts (overnight team, round 1)

To make a 5-minute session feel like progress beyond the haul, three contracts are always posted at the ledge and settled on coming home. They pay the star and a useful material, and steer expeditions (a biome, a zone, a creature, a rift) without forcing them. Falling settles nothing, so they add to the push-your-luck. No timers and no daily resets, so a short session never wastes one.

## 2026-10-18: round 2 (overnight team)

From the lead designer's measured review and the QA pass:
- **The lair is a fair finale.** The pull holds while the Keeper lives; it rages at 90 s; when it falls, its lodeline lights with a 30 s pull. Keepers have 30% less health. Before this, no Keeper fell in 210 bot dives: the fight used up the whole pull and the lodeline stayed dark.
- **Lodelines stay a gamble.** Odds by site of 100/80/60/40%, +5% a magnitude, capped at 100/90/75/55%. The star's growth now softens the recall instead (−2% lost a magnitude). Before, from magnitude 3 every site had one and the push-your-luck vanished.
- **The beacon at magnitude 2,** where it matters, and never over a lit lodeline. Tool costs take parts of any grade.
- **Contracts keep moving:** no duplicates, mostly in the newest biome, stale ones replaced after 3 expeditions, a free swap each time home, a bonus for the whole board.
- **Imprinting** (the designer's crafting proposal): legendaries salvaged at home teach the forge their power; a heart or 2 riftglass imprints it on a new piece. It turns lucky finds into recipes and gives hearts and riftglass a purpose.

## 2026-10-18: portals, not holes

The designer found that holes down to the next site didn't make sense now that a way on can lead up to the surface, and suggested portals.
- **Every way on is a portal:** a standing ring of lodestone on the site's exit tile, its face swirling in its destination's colour (warm daylight for the surface, cool blue for a cave, amber for a ruin, red for a Keeper's Lair, green for a Deep Vein), with the zone's glyph on its keystone in place of the old signpost. Dark stone, and colours that are never violet, magenta or cyan, keep it apart from a rift's tears; the Deep Vein's sign moved from violet to green for the same reason.
- **Stepping through is a moment, not a fall.** The world holds still for 0.45 s while the view closes on the portal's face in its colour, then opens round the diver at the next site. No shake, as ever.
- **The words follow.** The portal's button is STEP THROUGH: and its zone; the rift gate's became ENTER THE TEAR, so the two never share words. Wick says "Through we go".
- **The outpost sets out through a portal too,** where the sinkhole's ledge was. Every expedition starts on the sunlit surface, so jumping into the pit and landing under the sky made no sense. The pit stays: the star hangs over it, and its lodelines still run down into it and bring you home.
- **Generation is untouched.** A portal stands on the tile the shaft did (renamed TILE_PORTAL, the same value), so the golden hashes didn't change.

## 2026-10-18: UI polish, from the designer's feedback

- **A kinder anvil.** The designer found forging too hard. The marker now sweeps slower (0.75, 0.95, 1.15 sweeps a second, from 0.9, 1.25, 1.6) and drags to 75% speed while it's over the zone; the zone is wider and narrows less (28% of the bar, 3% less a strike, from 26% and 4%); the perfect middle is marked above and below and is 25% of the zone (from 30%, since the drag already widens it). In time, the good window is about 500, 350 and 250 ms by strike (from 290, 175 and 110) and the perfect one about 125, 90 and 65 ms (from 85, 55 and 35). In a simulation with a typical phone player's timing, most strikes now land good or better (about 90%, from about 65%), while three perfects in a row stay rare (about 3–8%, from under 2%). So a fine forge is the usual result and a masterwork is still earned.
- **A header bar with the way out.** The designer found leaving a screen by a BACK button at the bottom, often scrolled out of sight, not great. Every panel (the Meridian, SET OUT, the forge, gear and the rune bench, the star, cargo) now has a header held at the top while it scrolls: the title on the left, a 48 px ✕ on the right, padded clear of the notch. The item card has the same: a bar with where the piece is, KEEP and ✕, with the rest scrolling under it, and a tap on the dark round the card closes it too. The bottom BACK buttons are gone. What moves you on stays in reach at the bottom instead: FORGE as before, and now the report's CONTINUE. Esc closes an open card first, then the screen.
- **Every cost says what it takes.** The designer couldn't tell what a reroll spends. Rune work is paid in the ore of the piece's own tier; the card now says so above the affixes (the ore's icon, its name and how many you have), and each REROLL, RAISE and ADD AN AFFIX button names its cost, or why it can't be done ("need 4 Lumenite, have 2", "fully raised"). The forge's FORGE button lists everything it will take, the exotic and the imprint included, or what's missing; the imprint says which it takes (a heart or riftglass) and the tools at SET OUT show each material with its icon and how many you have, in red when short.

## 2026-10-18: climes

The designer asked for more variety in the environment, "completely different biomes", and chose climes per site.
- **The region sets the ore and the danger; the clime sets the place.** Each site rolls one of 11 climes for its zone (4 on the surface, 4 for caves and Deep Veins, 3 for ruins): its own ground, rock and light, flora, critters, weather and one hazard. Keepers' Lairs keep their own look, so the finale stays a clean fight.
- **Today's looks are each zone's first clime** (Badlands, Caverns, Archive), with no hazard, so nothing is lost and their floors generate exactly as before (the golden hashes held).
- **Every clime can turn up in every region; regions only lean** (2–2.5×): ice towards the Choir, ash and magma towards the Underforge, fen and roots towards the Rim. With equal weights, an expedition of four sites nearly always meets something new.
- **Rolled with the way, not on arrival,** from the expedition's seed, so the portal's button names the place (STEP THROUGH: FROST STEPPE) and the route is a choice of country. It's saved with the run, and a resumed site rebuilds in the same clime.
- **A small shared set of hazards, not one-offs:** pools that slow, ice that slides, lava that burns, vents that burst, spore clouds that sting. Climes share them in their own colours (the Grotto's water and the Temple's pools; the Cinder Waste's fire vents and the Vault's steam). Each is mild, shown before it bites, crossed unharmed by a dash, kept out of the entry's cavern and away from portals and lodelines, and lava never cuts any ground off. Walking enemies wade and burn too, so it's fair both ways. The goal is variety, not difficulty. Over 24 bot careers (576 dives) each, 31.4% of dives ended in a fall on main and 35.4% with climes, while the two halves of each varied by about 5 points; the hazards dealt under 1% of the damage the bot took. The bot was taught to walk round lava and vents, while still seeing what stands on them.
- **Hazards are laid after the layout from their own generator,** like rifts, so a clime changes the tiles only where it means to (fewer pillars on the steppe, more in the waste). A clime never adds caverns or makes them bigger or more open: an early version did, and the bot fell more often there, from the enemies rather than the hazards.
- **Flora never looks like ore, and walls always read as walls:** no crystal shapes among the new plants (the geode idea was dropped for that reason), rock tops always darker than the floor, glows in colours no ore uses, and portal motes in a clime's colour but never violet, magenta or cyan, which belong to rifts.
- **Grafts bring a clime too:** a rift's graft is a patch of another region's ground in one of its climes, such as a patch of steppe snow in a cave.
- **Drawn for the A+ move:** few tones a material, hue-shifted ramps (violet shadows, warm lights), clusters rather than speckle, no dither on props. The art stays at 16 px until the migration.

## 2026-10-18: the art direction, "A+ Midway"

The designer compared several looks for the art's next step, drawn by a mock-up renderer at 1×, 1.5× and 2×, and chose A+ Midway.
- **Plain 2× was rejected.** Doubling the resolution and keeping the old shading (smooth gradients dithered over every tone, noise speckle) "moved away from pixel art": it read as a smoothed painting, not as clusters of pixels.
- **A+ is a set of pixel artist's rules, not a resolution:**
  - at most 3–4 tones of a material on any one thing, picked by its form lit from the top left rather than spread as a gradient;
  - hue-shifted ramps: shadows lean to violet, lights to warm gold, middle tones a little richer;
  - texture in clusters of light and dark, never per-pixel speckle or orphan pixels;
  - no dithering on creatures, sprites or props; a little only at the edges of large ground gradients and of the light's steps;
  - black outlines on creatures (the diver, enemies, critters) and sel-outs (a much darker shade of the colour inside) on flora and props;
  - light in a few hard bands, and the bloom kept.
- **Three steps:**
  1. Apply the rules to today's 16 px art. Done: the sprite builder shades by form, the ground is clustered and the light banded, with every knob in `T.art`.
  2. Move to 24 px tiles by enlarging the letter-row material grids 1.5× before shading, so the shading adds detail at the new size instead of blurring the old (`T.art.px` scales the painters' distances).
  3. Redraw by hand where the enlarged art falls short.
- **The rules that don't change:** walls read as walls (rock tops darker than the floor, lit rims and faces), flora never looks like ore (the ores' ramps are never hue-shifted), no screen shake, and the art is visual only, so generation and the golden hashes are untouched.
- **Critters are outlined now,** as creatures. They stay muted and small, so they still read as part of the place rather than as enemies.
- **The light keeps its colour at a step.** Banding each colour channel on its own shifted a tinted light's hue (the badlands went red), so the steps are of brightness only, and flat ambient light is snapped to a whole step so it never sits on a dithered edge.

## 2026-10-18: 24 px tiles (A+ step 2)

The art moved from 16 to 24 px a tile; play didn't change.
- **Two units.** The world stays in world px: `TILE` is 16, and every body, reach, speed, radius and tuning number is as it was, so generation, the golden hashes and the balance are untouched. The art is drawn in art px, `T.art.px` (1.5) to each world px, 24 (`ATILE`) to a tile. Anything drawn goes from world to art as its world position × 1.5 less the camera, in whole art px; the camera itself sits on a whole art px, so nothing jitters against the floor.
- **Floors are painted at 24 px natively.** The painters take the art tile size; every distance in them (the wall faces, shadows, the sun's slant, flagstones, ore shards, decor) is its old value × 1.5, rounded, and their noise is read in world px, so a pattern is as big as it was and gains finer edges, not a finer grain.
- **Sprites are enlarged as letter grids, before they're shaded,** never as pictures: Scale3x (AdvMAME3x), then every other row and column. Each odd pixel keeps its two corners and each even one its middle, so diagonals stay clean steps, curves stay round and nothing is blurred or invented; then the A+ shader and outliner run at the new size, so the shading is real and outlines stay one pixel. What glows (eyes, runes, cores, visors) is enlarged as exact blocks instead, neither rounded off nor spilling, so small lights keep their shape. The bigger sprites (the Hollow, the warden, the Lodestar) are enlarged the same way at 3× or 4.5×. No sprite is ever drawn with a non-whole scale.
- **Particles, sparks, bolts, rings and the numbers stay one art pixel,** which makes them finer; shapes drawn in dots that stand for things (the drone, loot gems, held weapons, the lanterns) were scaled with the rest. The label and number fonts keep their glyphs, a pixel each.
- **Light and bloom are in world terms:** the light grid's cells are 3 art px (2 world px, as before), radii are world px × 1.5, and the bloom's passes are shrunk by 1.5 more, so a glow spreads as far as it did.
- **The view's size.** At least 176 world px on the shorter side, as before, but with whole device pixels to an art px the next scale up is taken when it still shows nine tenths of that (160 world px), so a phone with 2 device px to a CSS px isn't shown a third more of the world with smaller sprites.
- **The sunlit ground is calmer** (`T.art.sunGround`): its dark and light clumps are drawn halfway to the middle tone and fewer of them, so the badlands' sand no longer reads as camouflage and what stands on it stands out. Wall and mesa shadows keep the full dark tone.
- **Cost.** 2.25 times the pixels. Painting a floor is about 1.5 times what it was (the ground's texture is worked out a row at a time from a lattice hashed once a floor, and the cave painter reads the rock in place); a frame about 1.3 times at the median and cheaper at the worst (the light is worked out once a cell and written a whole pixel at a time; the portal's swirl a block at a time).

## 2026-10-18: flora grows into the place

The designer found the flora "obviously plopped down" and wanted it to look integrated, as in merryfolk. Each floor tile used to roll on its own and drop one weighted piece on bare ground.
- **Placed by habitat and in drifts, the way merryfolk plants its valley.** A tile's suitability comes from how near a wall it is, its nooks, standing water and a clime's damp, lava or daylight underground, and openness (the middles of open ground keep only a quarter of it, so fights have room). Where anything grows is one seeded noise; which kind grows is each kind's own, so drifts are mostly of one kind with bare ground between, fraying at the edges, with an outlier now and then. Big pieces are spaced by their size, and small ones gather round them and along the walls.
- **Rooted, not stamped.** Under and round every plant, stand and run of growth along a wall's foot, the ground is painted into the floor's pixels as a bed: the ground's own clumps drawn towards the clime's soil, moss, litter, ash or thaw, with a ragged edge that follows those clumps, strewn with leaves or flecks, a clustered contact shadow to the lower right, and tufts, sprigs, moss, roots and pebbles. Walls take part: moss climbs a face over a bed at its foot, roots, vines or icicles hang from lips underground, scree lies at the foot in the rock's own colours, and under the sky a mesa's lip has a ragged fringe, like merryfolk's hanging grass.
- **Stands of tall grass and reeds** hide the diver and creatures to the knees or the waist. They're drawn in rows sorted with the actors. Their stems are a pixel wide, set dark at the foot and light at the tips, with no sel-out, which on stems that fine would be all outline. The rest of the new ground cover is letter rows shaded and sel-outed like any flora.
- **Moving.** Under the sky everything leans in a wind of swells and gusts (merryfolk's windAt and blitSway): rows shifted by whole art px, the top most. Underground there's no wind, but a plant stirs as the diver or a creature brushes past. Two-frame pieces keep their sway or pulse underground; under the sky the wind replaces it.
- **Still cosmetic, still render-side.** The placement uses the floor's seed through its own hash, after the rock's pixels are known and before the tiles are painted, so generation, collision and the golden hashes are untouched. A mined rock's ground stays bare, as it would be fresh, and a piece hanging from or growing up a wall that's mined away goes with it.
- **The fighting flora blend in.** They're placed by the game as before; they get a bed, a shadow and cover like any plant, and one or two harmless pieces of the shape they wear grow beside them.
- **The rules hold.** Ground cover and beds use the flora's and the clime's own colours, never an ore's; pebbles are the rock's face colours with no glints; rock tops stay darker than the floor and keep their lit rims.
- **Cost.** At phone size with the CPU slowed 4×, painting a floor takes about 10–15% longer (beds and the wall's growth are worked out a row at a time, reusing the cave painter's texture and face depths) and a frame about 1 ms more at the median (what's out of view is skipped, stands are drawn a row of up to two tiles at a time from one picture, and only what may stand over something glowing cuts out its glow).
- **From the review.** Stands under the sky are olive with sun-bleached tips, never blue. Sky-grass is redrawn as a soft leafy sage clump, and the Grotto's barnacles as lobed clay sponges (the mound keeps a glowing pore, which goes red on a spitter); its borrowed dripstone gives way to a beard of moss, and its anemones are coral with pink tips, all clear of every ore's colour and with no facets or spikes. A damp clime now only sways which kinds grow, not how much, so its open ground stays as clear as any (open ground starts 3 tiles off a wall); stands keep within 2 tiles of a wall unless by water, and never cross the middle of a passage under 4 tiles wide, so what comes along it stays in sight.

## 2026-10-18: redrawn by hand at 24 px (A+ step 3)

Where the automatic enlargement fell short, sprites were redrawn by hand at the art's size.
- **How they're marked.** A grid drawn at the art's size is wrapped in `artNative(rows)` (art.js), which records the scale it's drawn at; `buildArtSprite` (and the critters' builder) enlarges it only by what's left, so a native grid isn't put through `scale15Rows` at all, and one drawn at 1.5 used for a sprite twice the size (the Hollow, the warden) is enlarged ×2 by Scale2x. Each is drawn to exactly the size its old grid enlarged to (ceil(1.5 w) × ceil(1.5 h)), centred, its feet on the same row, so anchors, hand points, hitboxes and generation are unchanged.
- **Redrawn:** the diver (every facing and walk frame), the rift's tears, the crawler, wisp and construct (and so the Hollow and the warden, built from them), the Rim Keeper, the critters, the lanterns, the blueprint icon and the drone.
- **The diver's circuits are lines of glow a pixel wide:** a V down the chest, a line down each arm and each shin, never alternating dots, which the enlargement turned into blocks. Its walk is a clean four-step cycle: a foot down in front, passing (the body a pixel higher, the other foot lifted), the other foot down, passing; walking towards the camera the forward foot is lower, walking away it's higher, and the arms swing against the legs. Seen from the side, the far leg is a shade darker (`v`).
- **Legs and antennae a pixel wide are drawn in a mid tone of the creature's shell (`j`), never black,** so the black outline frames them as lines instead of swallowing them. The big Keeper's legs are two pixels wide.
- **The rift's tear is ragged but crisp:** its rim runs in straight steps between set points, with a few barbs torn off it, never in pixel-by-pixel jaggies; its core is a crack of light in straight runs.
- **Speckle became structure:** the Rim Keeper's shell is two plate seams and a few pits rather than a scatter of dark dots; its heart-shard is a faceted crystal, lit on its left, in a socket of star metal.
- **The fighting flora are untouched,** so their disguises are still built from the same rows as the plants they wear.
- **Cost.** A frame costs the same; building the sprites is about a fifth faster, as the native grids aren't enlarged.

## 2026-10-18: the flora's feet melt into the ground

The designer found that the bushes and trees still had "a very clear separation from the ground": each plant sat on its bed as a cut-out, its sel-out running along a flat bottom edge, its contact shadow a floating oval off to one side. Merryfolk's plants read as growing out of the ground because their foot is dark and close to the ground's own colours, grass overlaps it in front, and roots spread from it.
- **No sel-out at the foot.** A standing plant's lowest 3 art px have no outline (`buildArtSprite`'s `openFoot`), so the base isn't ringed. Hanging pieces keep theirs.
- **The foot is shaded into the ground.** Up each column from its lowest pixel, a few whole-pixel steps are drawn towards the colour of the ground in the plant's shadow (its bed's deep tone, darkened), the steps' edge ragged in pairs of columns. It only ever darkens: a pull towards a lighter colour read as a ghostly fade on dark plants on snow. Worked out once a sprite and floor look, so drawing costs nothing more.
- **Blades in front.** Two to five little clumps of blades a pixel wide (the set's stand material, or pebbles in the Magma Tubes, where blades would be rock) grow over each plant's foot (and over a piece's at a wall's foot), at its corners and between on a wide one, and lean with it. They're drawn right after the plant, from the stands' picture, not as items of their own, to keep the frame's cost down.
- **Roots and skirts.** Trees, big stalks, mushrooms, stumps and bushes spread short roots a few px into the floor from each side of the foot, in the darkest and middle colours of their own base; every plant may get a stamp of its bed's growth tight against each side.
- **A tight contact shadow,** centred under the foot's own span (not the sprite's width), a little to the lower right.
- **The fighting flora match.** Disguised snapjaws and spitters are built with the same open foot, melted the same way when drawn, and given the same blades, roots and shadow, so the base doesn't give them away. The soft oval shadow other creatures get is no longer drawn under them, since no real plant has one.
- **The Frost bulb is a plant.** It was a pale blue faceted crystal on a snow base, which read as ore; now it's a rounded leafy bulb in the frost flora's dark teal, rimed at its leaves' edges, with a small bud at its tip (red on a snapjaw).
- **Cost.** At phone size with the CPU slowed 4×, painting a floor costs the same within noise, and a frame about 0.5–1 ms more at the median (about half of it the blades in front: one more small picture a plant in view).

## 2026-10-18: casting looks like the diver doing it

The designer found the magic "bolts of stuff" that "appear out of nowhere", and wanted movement on the diver and effects round it and the ground.
- **Casting stays instant.** The designer chose this: a spell still fires on the frame of the tap, so timing, damage, reach and balance are as they were. Everything new is cosmetic and wraps round that instant; a held charge already takes a while, so that time now shows a build-up.
- **A cast pose, drawn by hand.** In each facing the diver thrusts an arm out at the aim for 0.2 s, its body leaning a pixel into it and back: from the side and three-quarter views the arm on the aim's side, from the front and the back the casting hand's own (the other hand's pose is the same one mirrored onto the other arm, the dome and antenna left as they are). The pose turns the diver to the aim for its length. It never blocks moving or anything else: walking, the legs come from the walk frames under the pose. The styles differ by the focus: a wand's orb flicks out and back, a staff lifts its head and swings it down at the aim (it's now drawn as a shaft with the orb at its head), a gauntlet punches, leaning further, with its knuckles glowing and a ring knocked off them. Charging holds the pose, gathering.
- **Bolts leave the fist.** Each starts at the fist (drawn from there, easing onto its true path in a few hundredths of a second; where it is in play doesn't change), with a flash and rays at the fist, a flare of light, and motes kicked out along the aim.
- **Round the diver:** a rune circle flashes under the feet and spreads, and motes rise round the body. (A ring pulsing off the body read as a hoop round the waist, and was dropped.) The big shapes do more: a ring swept over the ground (out for a nova or thorns, in for a pull or a well, closing round the diver for a ward), dust pushed along with it, the flora stirred as it passes, and a bloom of light on the walls and ground round about.
- **Bolts have life:** a trail, a head that flickers and turns (a charged bolt is a turning star), a spark's wobble, a frost lance's glint; what each sheds is its element's (ice drifting down, cinders and smoke, sparks, drips); chains crackle, fork and flash at each end. Where one lands it bursts in its element's bits and leaves a mark on the ground that fades in a second or two (scorch with embers, a star of rime, a crazed burn, a green splash, a burnt rune).
- **A charge builds:** motes are drawn into the focus, a rune circle grows under the feet and flashes white when full, the focus burns brighter with a ring closing round it; the charged cast's burst is bigger, with a ring swept out.
- **Blink and phase** leave afterimages in their colour along the way, and a flash and pillar of light at each end. **An Echo's recast** shows a ghost of the pose and its circle, and its bolts leave the ghost's fist. Split's three bolts each kick their own motes out of the fist, so the fan shows.
- **Keyed by shape and element, not by spell.** A spell's look about the diver comes from its shape (projectile, close sweep, burst round the diver, placed where it lands, move, guard: `T.castFx.shapes`, through one entry point, `castFxFor`) and its element's colour, motes and mark (`CFX_ELEMENTS`), so spells built later from other parts take the look of their shape and element. Today's spells name their shape in `T.castFx.shapeOf`.
- **Pixel art, no shake.** Rings and glyphs are rasterised ellipses a pixel wide, painted once and kept; motes are single pixels; fades are in four hard steps; nothing is a smooth gradient or drawn at a non-whole scale. Nothing shakes the view or flashes the whole screen; the light that flares is local.
- **In daylight the pixels carry it, not the light.** Under the sky by day a cast's lights (its flares, a charging focus, bolts and what they hit) bloomed into a soft cloud that washed out the diver and the ground. There each keeps a third of its radius, overlapping ones are merged, and none lightens the ground by more than one band. Underground and at dusk they're as they were. Motes are capped (`T.castFx.cap`), and use `Math.random`, never the dive's generator.
- **Cost.** At phone size with the CPU slowed 4×, in a stress fight (30 enemies that never die, a staff casting split, echoing bolts and a gauntlet's novas into them all the while), a frame costs about 1–2.5 ms more at the median (about 19.5 → 21 ms, runs interleaved with main's, which varied by 1.5 ms among themselves) and a step the same. What keeps it there: rings over everyone are queued dots; those on the ground are pictures painted once (their radii stepped by 2 past 16 px, so there are fewer); at most 180 motes, 16 marks and 10 flaring lights at once.

## 2026-10-18: shadows from the lamp

The designer asked for lighting and shadows from the diver, since light and kindling are at the heart of the game, and chose walls plus soft prop shadows.
- **The rock stops the lamp.** Rays are marched out from under the dome through the floor's rock pixels (the smooth wall line, so a shadow follows the wall, not the tiles); a cell of the light's grid that's further than its ray went gets none of the lamp. The shadow falls to the ambient light, never to black, so the diver can always see enough to fight, and it's cut in the light's own hard bands. The sight is worked out again only when the diver moves a cell, the lamp's reach changes or the rock does, so a mined wall stops casting at once.
- **Walls still read as walls.** The rock a ray strikes keeps a lit skin (`reach`), and a wall's front face is lit wherever the ground under it is lit and the lamp is south of it, so a face the diver looks at never goes dark, however low the light strikes it. Rock tops the lamp can't reach fall to ambient (`top` 0), which only makes them darker than the floor. The sinkhole stops nothing.
- **Other lights.** The floor's fixed lights (lava, vents, glowing plants, a temple's shaft) are stopped by the rock too, worked out when they're baked and kept, so mining redoes only those that reach the mined tiles. Ore glows from inside the rock and is lit as before, so its twinkle and glow always read; daylight is ambient and casts nothing. Moving lights (spells, shots, enemies' eyes) and the lodeline's beam, which comes down from the sky, aren't shadowed.
- **Soft prop shadows.** Big plants (trees, giant mushrooms, columns, tall stalks), enemies, the warden and portals cast a short shadow on the ground pointing straight away from the lamp, from the foot, a little longer the further they stand, capped at about a tile. It's two hard steps (a rim and a core) with a ragged edge in clusters of 2 art px fixed to the world, multiplied over the ground before anything stands on it, so the telegraphs, eyes, cores and loot drawn later stay readable over it; a step goes as the lamp's share of the light at the foot falls, so in daylight and far from the lamp there's none. Small plants, critters and loot cast none.
- **Visual only.** Enemies' sight, bodies, hitboxes, generation and the golden hashes are untouched.
- **Cost.** At phone size with the CPU slowed 4×, the diver wandering a cave, a ruin and the surface at night (five runs each, interleaved with main's): a frame's median is 0.1–0.4 ms more and the 95th percentile 0.3–0.6 ms more, while the worst frames are within noise. The lamp's sight is worked out only when the diver moves a cell (about 0.9 ms then, at 4×): a window round the dome's disc, each ray marched only to the disc's edge, 2 art px a step, and straight across any block between four open tiles' middles. The props' shadows are greys multiplied over the ground, so the view's pixels are never read back (about 0.1 ms a frame). Baking the floor's lights takes about 40 ms more on entering a floor, and after mining only the lights that reach it are worked out again.


## 2026-10-18: circuit spells replace melee and spell cores

The designer read the Spellcraft proposal and said "I like the circuit spell system, let's go with that". This is its first phase.
- **All magitech, all spells, close range included.** Blades, the spear, hammer and whip, the three-hit swing, the spin, the "cores fire every third hit" rule and the Might attunement are gone. Each hand holds a **caster** (a wand, a gauntlet or a staff) whose row of slots holds **stones**, read left to right: a Form starts a spell, Elements after it set its damage type (two at most, the damage split and both statuses applied; none is Force), Glyphs change the Form to their left, two Forms in a row fire together, Trigger makes the next Form fire from where this one hits or ends, and what comes before the first Form does nothing. A press casts the whole circuit; a hold charges it, as before.
- **Close range is a spell.** The Arc replaces the blade: three taps, the third heavy and staggering; held, a Nova of the same element. The Glaive replaces thorns and the spin. Close Forms give charge back for each foe they hit, which replaces "melee refills charge faster" and keeps the close-and-far rhythm. Might became **Proximity**: close Forms +8% a rank. The melee affix family became the close family (close spell damage, cast speed, crits), and crits now land on any spell.
- **The designer's calls:** a resonance must fill its caster exactly; launch with a core set and grow it (8 Forms: Arc, Glaive, Bolt, Lance, Orb, Nova, Well, Ward; 5 Elements: Force, Shock, Frost, Ember, Blight; 8 Glyphs: Split, Amplify, Quicken, Chain, Echo, Pierce, Linger, Trigger; 6 resonances listed, for phase 3); and **saves start fresh**, with no conversion: the save key moved on, an old save is left where it was, and a player who had one is told in one line before the opening.
- **The casters.** A wand has 3–4 slots and is quick (15% less wait); a gauntlet 3–4, and close Forms cost it 1 charge less; a staff 4–6, slower (30% more) with every reach 30% wider. The anvil's temper sets the slots within the base's range. A staff's charged cast no longer fires three times: every caster charges the same way, so the base is a choice of slots, speed and reach.
- **Stones are forged.** A Form's stone needs its blueprint (Arc and Bolt from the start; the other six found as core blueprints were) and is bound with ether. An Element's comes from its exotic (Force from ore alone), and a caster forged with an elemental exotic brings its stone out with it. A Glyph's is forged from creature parts: chitin makes Pierce and Split, ether Echo, cogs Quicken, fibre Linger and Trigger, spores Chain, and any part Amplify. A stone's tier is its ore's (one more from a fine forge); a Form stone's tier adds 25% a tier, an Element's or Glyph's 5%.
- **Socketing is free at home,** at the locker: tap a caster, a slot, a stone. The card says in plain words what the circuit does and names its cost in charge. A caster equipped with nothing in it takes over the circuit of the one it replaces, so a better base never means rebuilding by hand.
- **Charge was halved to fit the proposal's costs** (30, refilling 4.5 a second; Max charge affixes halved too), so a Bolt (4) and a Nova (10) are the share of the pool that a Bolt (8) and a Nova (18) were.
- **Starting kit:** a Scrap Gauntlet `ARC · – · –` and a Cracked Wand `BOLT · – · –`. The hands' buttons show each circuit's first Form.
- **Looks.** Each Form takes a cast shape (castfx.js): the Arc is the `sweep`, a crescent of light swept across in front in its element's colour, white at its head, the heavy third thicker and wider and throwing sparks; the Glaive's blades are short crescents turning round the diver; the Orb is a ball with a white heart that rings when it pulses; a Linger's field is its element's colour on the ground. Stones are pixel icons drawn at the art's size: a pebble for a Form, a cut gem for an Element, a tablet for a Glyph, in its ore's colours, its rune glowing.
- **Left for later, with hooks in the engine:** reactions between statuses (`circuitReact`), resistances and weaknesses (`elementMult`), ore affinity (`affinityMult`), linked slots, resonances and the Codex (`circuitResonance` finds them; nothing acts on one yet), and the Lode and Rift elements with more Forms and Glyphs.
- **Balance held.** The autopilot was taught circuits: an Arc in the gauntlet with Quicken and an element, a Lance (or a Bolt) in the other hand with Split or Pierce, Amplify and Echo as slots allow, forging the stones it wants from what it carries home. Over 20 careers of 24 dives each, 30.0% of dives ended in a fall (main: 31.0%), 51.9% came home by a lodeline (50.2%) and 16.5% by a recall (17.3%); careers ended at magnitude 4.0 on both, and went a little further along an expedition (2.76 sites, from 2.51). The pick ended a little lower (tier 3.5, from 4.2), because the bot now forges two casters and stones from the same ore.
- **Cost.** In the stress fight at phone size with the CPU slowed 4× (30 enemies that never die, a staff casting split, echoing bolts, a gauntlet's novas), a frame's median is the same within noise (15.3–20.1 ms over three interleaved rounds, main's 16.9–19.5) and a step about 0.1 ms more at most (0.9–1.1 ms).
- **From the review.** A struck enemy no longer turns into a white silhouette: on the glow layer it bloomed into a cloud that hid the spell, the enemy and the numbers, worst when a Nova or an Arc struck a crowd. It now wears a rim a pixel wide, white or in the hit's element, on the unlit layer over the view, so it's never a light and never blooms, the enemy stays in sight inside it, and no more than four show at once (`T.impact.flashMost`). Stones are coloured by what they are, never by their ore, so none looks like ore or loot: a Form a plain stone pebble with a gold rune, an Element a gem in its own colours (Force pale violet-white, Shock yellow, Frost ice blue, Ember orange, Blight green), a Glyph a teal tablet; the tier is written beside it. Hints wait while a screen is open, and one showing when a screen opens comes back when it closes, so none lies over a panel.

The designer asked for "biome specific enemies" and chose a signature enemy for each clime: its own creature, its own attack and its own elemental nature, while the crawler, wisp, construct and the fighting flora stay as the common foes everywhere.
- **Eleven creatures, each tied to its clime and its hazard** (DESIGN.md, Enemies, has the table): the Dune Skink swims under the Badlands' sand; the Tusker slides on the Frost Steppe's ice into the rock; the Ash Wraith rises from the Cinder Waste's fissures and leaves the ground burning; the Bog Lurker sits in the Bloomfen's mire and drags; the Ceiling Dropper falls in the Caverns; the Pool Eel swims the Grotto's pools; the Slag Crab's shell and lava-proof legs suit the Magma Tubes; the Rootweaver strings roots across Rootdeep's narrow ways; the Sentinel Eye sweeps the Archive's halls, where pillars are cover; the Moss Idol moves only in the Temple's dark; the Gear Hound ricochets round the Vault.

Nowhere else, but a rift's graft brings one or two of its clime's (not the eel or the dropper). The Hollow, the dark's waves and the rifts' elites stay common. They take turns with the rest (the attackers' cap), elites roll as for any, and they grow with the level.
- **Every attack shows first, and a dash crosses it.** The tells use the crawler's red (lines are dashes two pixels long with a dark pixel under each, so they read on snow and sand), rings with `ringDots`, and a dropper's shadow growing on the floor; each creature's light swells as it winds up. - **Out of sight means out of reach.** A skink under the sand, an eel under the water, a dropper on the roof and a wraith in its fissure can't be struck, aimed at or chained to (`e.hid`), but each shows a sign (a mound with eyes, a fin and ripples, a smouldering crack) except the dropper, whose shadow is its tell. Each has a moment open to a counter after its attack (`T.enemies.exposed`, as a dazed construct).
- **Natures are data.** `T.enemies.resist` holds each one's weakness and resistance in the five core elements for the resistance system; the Slag Crab's shell is a rule of its own (`crabShell`, foes.js): a third of a blow gets through until it's chilled, or a heavy hit cracks it for 5 s.
- **The Moss Idol is lit by the lamp as it's drawn.** It counts as lit within 0.6 of the lamp's reach where the lamp's own sight (shadow.js) reaches it, or in a temple's shaft of daylight; without a view (the autopilot), a line through the tiles. So a fading pull, which dims the lamp, makes idols bolder.
- **Looks.** Native 24 px letter grids drawn by hand (`artNative`), shaded by the sprite builder with black outlines, at most four hue-shifted tones a material, no dither, glowing eyes and cores, in their clime's colours and shapes unlike the trio, the flora or ore. The Tusker, the Gear Hound and the Moss Idol are drawn side on and mirrored to face their way (a lit idol keeps the way it faced); the rest face the view as the trio do. Legs a pixel wide go in short steps, never long diagonals, which read as a checker pattern.

Lit, it's a grey statue with dark cracks and eyes; in the dark it prowls low with its cracks faintly alight, and gathers its haunches to pounce with its cracks and eyes ablaze. Its glow is a sour green, not an ore's teal, and the light it casts is pale, so in the dark its body still reads as stone. What it does and its numbers are unchanged.

## 2026-10-18: weaknesses, reactions, ore affinity, linked slots and resonances

Phases 2 and 3 of the Spellcraft proposal, which the designer approved, with their calls: a resonance must fill its caster exactly, and the core set (5 Elements, 8 Forms, 8 Glyphs, 6 resonances).

- **Every kind has a nature** (natures.js): weak (×1.5), resistant (×0.5) or very resistant (×0.25) to elements, never immune; the wisp resists Force (the designer's call: Force is every spell's element when it has none, so the crawler and the construct, which the proposal had resisting it too, are neutral to it), the plants Frost and Blight, the Hollow everything (but not in a lit lodeline's light, standing in for "kindled light" until there is any); the warden and the Keepers one of each by region. A clime moves its creatures a step (`T.climes[id].resist`), and four new elite affixes join in: Insulated, Fireproof, Warded (a shell of pale light that three Force hits crack) and Shifting (its weakness moves every 6 s). One function, `elementMultFor(enemy, element)`, applies all of it. **The data:** a kind's verdicts are `T.enemies.resist[type]` (the per-kind tables are `T.enemies[type]`, with no `types` table, so this is the fallback the plan named; `natureTable` also reads `T.enemies.types[type].resist` or `T.enemies[type].resist`, so a new kind can put its own beside its numbers), the multipliers `T.circuits.resist`, a clime's shift `T.climes[id].resist = { more, less }`, an affix's own `T.enemies.eliteResist`.
- **Readable, and learned.** Wick learns a kind's verdict on an element from its first hit, kept in the save (`natures`). Over each awake foe: a 3 art px mark for each verdict known, ▲ in the element's colour for weak, ▼ for resistant, a bar for very resistant, and "?" while some are unknown, three at most (what it fears first, then what it resists most); a hidden plant shows none until it wakes. A weak hit's number is a bold 5 × 7 hand in green, a resisted one the small hand in grey. **Uncluttered, after the review:** WEAK or RESIST under it at most once every 0.8 s over a foe, and never for a hit whose reaction's name shows; a reaction's name stands over the foe above where its numbers rise to, the next one stacked above it. Wick says so the first time each happens.
- **Reactions** (reactions.js), on from the start: each element's status lasts 3 s, and a second element on a carrier reacts in either order (Shatter, Conduct, Steam, Brittle, Pyre, Flare, Scatter, Jolt); an element on itself stacks (Overload, Freeze, Inferno, Plague, Stun); two elements on one Form react on their own. The old rule "a heavy frost hit on the chilled shatters" became Shatter. A reaction can't fire on the same foe again for 0.6 s; Shatter and Steam spend the statuses they used, Pyre the poison. A boss takes a third of a stun, a freeze or a blinding, the Hollow none. Each name flashes once over its target in the stations' pixel hand, and Wick explains each the first time.
- **Ore affinity:** a caster's ore is the one it's named for (its tier's, so a riftglass forging takes the next ore's). Ferrite Force and cheaper Glaives, Lumenite Shock and 10% faster casts, Resonite stronger resonances, Aetherglass Frost and longer Lances, Sigilite Ember and stronger Glyphs, Starmetal a slot more than its base (a masterwork Starmetal staff has 7). Attuned spells hit 25% harder by their share of the spell's elements, and their statuses last half again as long. The caster's card names its attunement, and the readout says what the ore does.
- **Linked slots:** a masterwork caster has an even chance of one linked pair; a Glyph in either counts twice, in what it does and in what it costs (Trigger counts once). A gold chain joins the two tiles.
- **Resonances** (resonance.js) hit 50% harder, and each is its own spell, keyed through castfx.js's shapes and elements: Thunderhead's orb flies up and becomes a stormcloud that drifts over the nearest foe and strikes every 0.4 s; Shatterglass's echo lance shatters the chilled for double and flings shards either side; Kindling Arc's heavy third leaves a crescent of fire in front; Wildfire's three orbs leave burning trails that set plants alight and run through them (that part is only for show, `Math.random`, real time); Stormglaive's blades arc lightning to every foe within reach; Starfall's three bolts rise and meet as a falling star that leaves a crater, stuns and rings with light (a hitstop, no shake).
- **The Codex** is a fifth tab at the forge (named "the Codex · resonances", as the forge's Codex of imprinted powers already exists): each resonance a silhouette with its slot count, its known parts as tiles and "?" for the rest. Resonance stones show one part each: elites drop them (4%), they lie by an ancient forge (35%), and contracts pay them; they're knowledge, kept at once like an echo. Kindling Arc is known from the start, and building or casting one fills in its page. At the locker a circuit one stone off a resonance on a caster of its size says "Resonance stirs…" (naming it if its page is whole), and a live one wears a gold banner.

Most of what was left was the autopilot's own: resting a hand its target resists, it held the other hand's button down two turns running, which charged instead of tapping. The Slag Crab's shell stands in for its resistances while it's shut, so a Force or an Ember hit on it does 35%, not 17.5%.

## 2026-10-18: the world replaces the dive chain (Lodestar Frontier, stage 1)

The designer approved the Frontier proposal: swap the four-site chain for one persistent world that spreads out from the star, taken back a territory at a time. Their calls:
- **All in.** The world replaces the dive chain: no flag, and no Set Out biome list. The portal opens the world map instead.
- **Kindled ground is never lost.** A territory kindled is saved the moment it's taken. Optional dark tides on border territories come later (stage 2).
- **The Kindler story** is written by a separate stage. This one only uses the words its systems need (spire, kindle, dark, light, territory) and calls the story's hooks (`Story.say` with `firstSpire`, `firstKindled`, `firstDeep`, `ring2`, `ring3`, `theEdge`; `kindleLogLine` when there is one), so it works before and after that merges.
- **Saves start fresh again.** The save key moved on to v4 and a player with an older save is told in one line before the opening, as with circuits.

What was built, and why it's this way:
- **One world a save, in rings.** Rolled from a seed kept in the save, which otherwise keeps only what's been done to each territory. Ring 0 the outpost, ring 1 the Rim (7), ring 2 the Choir (10), ring 3 the Underforge (12), ring 4 the Edge (10, Underforge ore with more Starmetal and a darker sky; its own content later). Each territory is a cell of the world map (the nearest middle, borders wandering by noise); cells that share a border are neighbours, so the map and the walking agree. Within a ring, a territory further out is deeper (1 to 4), so the enemy levels run as the old sites' did (ring 1 levels 1–4, and so on).
- **Territories are surface sites** from the floor generator (`generateTerritory(worldSeed, id)`), so climes, hazards, undergrowth and rifts all apply, with an opening at the edge for each neighbour on the side facing it. Walking into one is the portal's moment (no shake), and you arrive at the opening back. The floor generator is untouched: every old golden hash held, and the territories have golden hashes of their own. A territory has up to one more vein a cavern than a site had, so walking dark land is worth mining (without it the bot brought home a third of main's ore; with two, its careers ran a third ahead of main's), and a cavern fewer: the passages to its openings make up the ground, so it has as much open ground and flora as a site, and costs the same to paint.
- **Your light is the old pull**, kept whole: its meter, the lamp dimming past half, the dark stirring, and the Hollow at nothing. It drains only in dark land (75 s fresh), and fills in kindled land and at home; it's one light for the whole sortie, so how far you go into the dark is the push-your-luck. Dark land is under a dim dusk and kindled land under daylight, so the state reads at a glance.
- **Spires are permanent lodelines.** A kindled territory's stands on its heart (a tile the generator already keeps clear), drawn new to the A+ rules: a lodestone obelisk in the portal's four tones, a sel-out, gold runes and a gold crystal glowing, the lodeline's beam rising from it. BANK is the old EXTRACT (1.5 s, broken by a step or a hit) and doesn't end the sortie; TRAVEL opens the map and banks first. Coming home feeds the star 8 × d² once a sortie, d the deepest dark land walked, as the ride home did; feeding it at every bank let a sortie that banked three times feed it three times, and the bot's careers ran to magnitude 6 where main's reach 4. A fall wakes you at the last spire you touched, so a fall far out doesn't throw the walk away. The first ring-1 territory, the gentlest, starts kindled by the outpost's own spire and has no spire of its own (only the outpost's exists at first), so the first sortie is gentle; the outpost's portal is its way out.
- **Two heart goals for now**, built from what the game had: the waystones (three channels like sealing, the last held for 20 s in its light while the dark sends one more every 8 s) and the tear at the heart (a graft or an ore wake, sealed as any). Then a 6 s channel plants the spire. The hold first sent two every 6 s, and the bot, with the gear it starts with, fell in most holds; one every 8 s keeps it a fight.
- **Deeps keep the old expeditions** as optional dungeons of 1–3 sites under some territories, at the territory's level, left by a lodeline or the last portal, back up to the territory. Your light drains down there too. The Keepers' lairs stay reachable for now at the bottom of the Deep under each of the first three rings' furthest territory, while its shard is missing, so the story can still be finished; their own place in the world is stage 3.
- **What carries on:** contracts (reach a ring, kindle a territory, go down into a Deep instead of reach a site and ride a lodeline home; a haul counts all that was banked on the way), the tools (the compass points to the spire or the way towards the nearest kindled land; the beacon calls a spire's light down to bank and travel from and to stand in while your light comes back), the Lodestorm, rifts, climes, hazards, the altar and magnitude (still the pocket slots; it no longer opens biomes).
- **The bot plays the world:** it picks the dark territory beside lit land that's at its edge (the hardest it's ready for, a level more after two good sorties there), sets out (walking from the outpost, or from a spire beside it), works the heart goal while it has the light and health, mines (along the way too), dives a Deep now and then once it has a better pick, turns back when its light is no more than the walk back, banks, pushes out once or twice more, and travels home.
- **Balance.** Over 20 careers of 24 sorties each (main's latest, over 40 careers of 24 dives: falls 32.3%, magnitude 3.92, pick tier 3.67, 20.1 kills a dive): 19.4% of sorties ended in a fall, 80.0% came home and 0.6% by a recall; careers ended at magnitude 4.30 with a pick of tier 4.45, at 21.0 kills a sortie, a haul of 26.8 and 7.4 territories kindled (median 7, from 0 to 16); 130 of 480 sorties went down into a Deep. Falls run 23–24% over the first sixteen sorties and 11% over the last eight, as more land is kindled. It's a little quicker and safer than main: kindled land beside the dark is a refuge (by design, the land next to a spire is the gentle kind), and the bot turns back when its light is no more than the walk to lit land. Two things brought it this close: a ring's depths spread evenly by rank (as first rolled, from where each stood in its band, some worlds had no gentle land beside the starter and one career in twenty never improved its pick; falls were 24.8%, magnitude 4.25, pick 3.65), and a vein fewer a cavern than first tried (with up to two more, magnitude 4.55 and pick 5.0).
- **Cost.** At phone size with the CPU slowed 4×, twelve runs alternating with main's: entering dark land (and its first frame) against main's first site at dusk, 1109 against 1051 ms at the median, 1.01 paired; walking it, a frame 13.5 ms on both (1.00). Entering kindled land at its spire against main's first site in daylight, 0.95 paired, a frame 1.02. As first built a territory was 6–15% slower to enter, with 15% more open ground from the passages to its openings (more to paint, more flora); a cavern fewer, narrower passages and no waystones drawn once the spire stands brought it level. The world's layout is worked out once a session (74 ms); the map opens in 80 ms the first time, redraws a frame in 0.1 ms, and lays its ground again in 15 ms when a territory changes.

## 2026-10-18: the Kindler

The designer approved the "Lodestar Frontier" proposal and its framing, the Kindler, and chose to rewrite the story now, alongside the map work. Stranded gave no reason to take ground and ended by leaving the world you built.
- **The premise.** You're a Kindler of the Lodestar Charter, who rides a seed-star down to a dead world, plants it, and carries its light outward spire by spire until a colony can land. This seed struck wrong, cracked and sank into the sinkhole; you and Wick climb out of the landing capsule onto the rim. It's a planned landing gone wrong, not a crash. The *Meridian* is the colony ship in orbit, not a wreck.
- **The turn, in ring 2.** The echoes are now logs of earlier Kindlers. They start hopeful, grow uneasy, and then show that the world wasn't dead: the dark is its own living night, the Hollow is what grows in it, and the Keepers are earlier seed-stars that went dark. Their shards strengthen your star so its light reaches further. Nothing says it outright (a Charter plate under a Keeper's shell, "ask whose night it is first").
- **Two endings, at the Edge.** Kindle the heart (a garden world, the Hollow gone) or leave it (the world keeps a night, the colony lives in the light, the Hollow keeps to the dark). Both are panel sequences, `playKindlerEnding('kindle' | 'leave')`, and play goes on after either. The choice is kept in `Save.data.kindlerEnding`.
- **The wreck became the Meridian uplink,** a relay mast with a dish at the same spot (the station keeps its id, `wreck`). It shows the landers the *Meridian* can send (one for every `T.story.landerEvery`, 3, territories kindled), the shards set and the echoes. Lighting the beacon became **calling the landers**: with all three shards set and ground for `T.story.callLanders` (5) landers. The call plays a short landing that ends on the Edge, still dark; the two endings belong to the Edge's heart, which the world doesn't have yet (stage 3), so nothing plays them until it does. (Saves start fresh with the world, so the old `beaconLit` is gone.) Under the shards, the uplink keeps **the log**: each territory kindled, in its line.
- **Settlers.** For each lander down, a settler, a tent and a lantern join the plaza, up to six (all of them once the colony's called). Settlers are cosmetic, small and muted, and each carries a little star-lamp. They stroll, and say a short line when the Kindler comes near; after `T.story.turnEcho` (5) echoes they've heard, and with names from the world they talk about places you've lit. The count comes from `storyKindledCount()`, which reads `Save.data.world.territories[id].state === 'kindled'` and takes names and climes from the world's layout; the starter doesn't count (the outpost's own light reaches it), and it's 0 with no world.
- **For the world branch.** New `Story.say` keys: `firstSpire` ("Kindlers don't conquer. We just carry the light a little further than it was."), `firstKindled`, `firstTide`, `firstDeep`, `ring2`, `ring3`, `theEdge` and `firstSettlers` (said on coming home once there's a settler). Helpers: `storyKindledCount()`, `storyLanders()`, `kindleLogLine(clime, name)` (one line for the log for each territory kindled, by clime, the same line for the same name), `playKindlerEnding(choice, done)`, `Story.canCall()` and `Story.callLanders(done)`.
- **Told the same way:** five opening panels, Wick's first-time lines, the star's words as it grows and as each shard is set, eight echoes. The panels are still words only; each has a comment saying what its art should show.

## 2026-10-18: light that holds still as you walk

The designer noticed everything seemed to gleam or shine as the diver moved. Recording the same walk seven ways found the causes: the bloom shrank the glow layer on a grid fixed to the screen, so small glows were caught or missed as the camera moved and their halos flared and vanished (the biggest part); the dither at each light band's edge popped on and off; band edges hopped a 3 px cell at a time; and the diver wobbled a pixel either side on a view an odd number of pixels across. The designer chose, from side-by-side clips, to fix all of it:
- **The bloom is world-fixed** (`T.light.bloomWorld`): the glow layer is halved twice on grids fixed to the world before the bloom passes, so a glow's halo holds still.
- **No dither at band edges** (`T.art.lightDither` 0): hard bands, nothing sparkling.
- **Band edges glide a pixel at a time** (`T.art.lampPerPixel`): each pixel's light is read off a slope through its cell, for every light, not only the lamp.
- **A steady camera** (`T.art.steadyCam`): the camera follows the diver's whole art pixel.
- **Cost:** about +2.8 ms a frame at phone size with the CPU slowed 4× (about 17%). Left as it was: lamp shadows on walls still step a cell, which is rare and local.

## 2026-10-19: the painted style, flora, props, ore and drops (branch `painted-flora`)

## 2026-10-19: the painted style, the rock and ground polished (branch `painted-rock`)
